# ResearchFabric Manifesto

Scientific continuity should not depend on remembering the right conversation.

We want research questions, sources, hypotheses, methods, evidence, terminology, failures, and decisions to remain legible after an agent is replaced, a provider changes, a machine is retired, or a project pauses. That requires durable research state and explicit authority—not a larger transcript archive.

Knowledge is not evidence. Knowledge is reviewed understanding that can guide work across projects. Evidence is a traceable result produced or collected under a stated method and interpretation boundary. Each needs a home, and movement between them must be deliberate.

Agents can accelerate inquiry. They can retrieve, compare, implement, execute, criticize, and synthesize. Their recommendation is still a recommendation. LLM output is not experimental evidence, and no agent acquires scientific or Owner authority by being useful.

Git helps because it makes compact state reviewable, attributable, and portable. A commit records a change; it does not validate a scientific claim. Bibliographic systems, instruments, simulation environments, and large-asset stores should remain authorities for the data they are designed to own.

ResearchFabric therefore favors a small, inspectable foundation: Markdown, structured metadata, Git, and direct native systems. New middleware must earn its place through a concrete need.

The Research Vault and Research Design Lab are separate on purpose. One cultivates reusable semantic knowledge. The other preserves executable project inquiry and evidence. Their relationship is a governed exchange, not automatic synchronization.

The fabric is durable because its strands remain distinguishable.
